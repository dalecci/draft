For Boys

A school built for the way boys actually learn.

Boys are quietly falling out of the education system. In Quebec, the seven-year secondary graduation rate is 86.9% for girls and only 78.2% for boys. That gap has barely narrowed in a generation. The pattern repeats in Florida, where the gender gap in post-secondary enrollment now exceeds twelve points in many cohorts.

01 / The diagnosis

Why the system is losing them.

Jordan Peterson has been one of the most direct voices on why this is happening, and his argument has four parts.

01 / SITTING

The sitting problem.

Boys are biologically primed for high levels of physical activity and rough-and-tumble play. Forcing them to sit quietly for seven hours a day almost guarantees disengagement and behavioral problems.

02 / CHALLENGE

The challenge vacuum.

Schools have become increasingly bureaucratic and risk-averse, stripping out the competitive, goal-oriented structures that historically pulled boys forward.

03 / PURPOSE

The purpose vacuum.

When boys are denied real challenge and clear purpose, they don't drift into mediocrity politely. They check out, act out, or simply leave.

04 / DISCIPLINE

The discipline answer.

The solution is not less structure but more. Discipline, mastery, and the channeling of natural aggression into competence and service to something larger than the self.

You have to teach a boy how to be useful, how to be competent, how to take on responsibility. That's what turns the chaos of adolescence into the engine of a productive life. Jordan Peterson · paraphrased from public lectures
02 / The structural answer

Read against the diagnosis, the Sport-Études design reads like a prescription.

  • 01 · Movement

    It breaks the sitting problem.

    Three hours of high-intensity training in the middle of the day. Not after-school. Not weekends. Inside the school day, when boys most need it.

  • 02 · Measurement

    It replaces the lack of challenge.

    Daily, measurable performance benchmarks. Skill targets you can track week over week. Strength numbers that move. Scrimmage tape you watch on Monday and answer for on Tuesday.

  • 03 · The ladder

    It fills the vacuum of purpose.

    A visible ladder. Varsity team, scholarship, national selection, professional pathway. Every boy knows exactly what rung he's on and exactly what the next one looks like.

  • 04 · Modeling

    It addresses the shortage of male role models.

    Experienced coaches who have competed at high levels themselves now teach competence by modeling it. The men in front of these boys are not theorists. They have done the thing.

  • 05 · Discipline

    The question of discipline is not negotiated.

    Academic performance is the condition of continued enrollment. Slip in the classroom, lose training hours. There is no version of the program where a boy gets to skip class and stay on the court.

03 / The proof

The Quebec data confirms the hypothesis.

The students most at risk of disengaging, adolescent and young-adult males, outperform the general population by the largest margins in the program.
