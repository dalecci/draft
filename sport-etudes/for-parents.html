For Parents

What you need to know.

Straight answers to the questions families ask first. If something isn't here, ask us directly — we'd rather have the conversation than send you a brochure.

Will my child fall behind academically?

No. The Quebec data is clear and consistent: Sport-Études students match or outperform the general population on every measured academic outcome. Continued enrollment is conditional on academic performance, which structurally protects against the dropout patterns that drive academy programs into disrepute.

See the outcomes data →

What hours will my child be in school?

Standard school day for academics in the morning. 15+ hours per week of structured basketball training inside the school day (afternoons). Mandatory supervised study hall and tutoring built into the daily schedule. Optional evening sessions for additional development.

See the typical day →

How is this different from IMG or Montverde?

IMG and Montverde are excellent at producing professional basketball players. They are athletics-first institutions. We are an academics-first institution that takes basketball seriously. The conditional-enrollment academic standard is the structural difference, and it's the reason our outcome data on the classroom side is what it is.

What about college recruitment?

Quebec Sport-Études alumni feature prominently in the 168 Canadians currently playing NCAA Division I men's basketball. The model is proven as a college-pipeline structure. Our program will support college recruitment with the same intensity as any prep program — while preserving NCAA academic eligibility from day one.

What does it cost?

Tuition information will be published when the program opens. Comparable Florida boarding programs (Montverde, IMG) charge $60K–$100K per year. Our pricing model will reflect our market position.

Are scholarships available?

Florida's Family Empowerment Scholarship – Educational Options (FES-EO) became universally available to Florida K-12 residents under HB 1 (2023). We will be working to qualify as a participating private school, which would make FES-EO funds available toward tuition for eligible families.
